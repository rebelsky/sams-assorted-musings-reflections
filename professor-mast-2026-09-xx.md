The other day, I was reading [a substack about Wendell Berry as writing teacher](https://writingteacher.substack.com/p/what-wendell-berry-taught-me-about). One of the sentence particularly resonated with me.

> At some point, I wrote a pithy little essay about body image, and Mr. Berry returned the paper with a C+ on it and a note that read: "This is just good enough that I wish it were better."

It got me thining about my courses with Gerald Mast, legendary film scholar [1]. I took a lot of courses with Mast. Let's see ... ENGL-285, _Sound Film Comedy_, in the Autumn of 1984 (B-), ENGL-280, _Introduction to Cinema 1_, in Winter 1985 (also a B-), ENGL-280, _Introduction to Cinema 2_ [3], in Spring 1985 (up to a B), and, finally, ENGL-486, _The American Musical in Film and Theatre_, in Winter 1986 (A). I thought I'd also taken _Silent Film Comedy_, but I seem to be mistaken.

Poor Professor Mast. He had to deal with me for four different courses. And, for the first three, he wrote essentially the same thing on every paper.  I don't recall his exact words, but they were something like "You have good ideas; it's too bad that you cant write."

I struggled with that comment. I'd gotten A's on my writing in high school. I even got an A on my paper in a seminar I took at BU in my senior year of high school. But Chicago's standards were different. Mast, and other instructors, were unwilling to put up with my excessive use of the passive voice. I wish I could recall what else was wrong.

I'd like to say that I worked on my writing. Perhaps I did. I certainly would have benefitted from something like the Writing Lab [4]. Chicago may have had one, but I didn't know about it. Still, my writing improved. My proudest moment as a student came wth my last paper for Mast. I'm not completely certain, but I recall it being about the use of physical comedy in the American musical. Consider, for example, the great Donald O'Connor's "Make 'em laugh" in _Singing in the Rain_. In any case, Mast's comment was as follows.

> Your writing has finally reached the level of your ideas. A-.

I believe I have that paper stashed somewhere amongst my thosands of files. Perhaps I'll find it again someday.

In my memory, I achieved competent writing for that paper through _The Little Red Schoolhouse_, otherwise known as ENGL-330, _Academic and Professional Writing_. However, I didn't take that course until the Summer of 1988. I credit that class with making me a much better writer. I still use Joe Williams's book when I teach writing.

Since then, I've improved my writing, in part, by teaching writing and by writing regularly. In the early days of the musings, I sometimes focused on issues of import, such as how to end an essay. I tried to pay attention to how others did so. At least for ending essays, I quickly learned that news reporters struggle, too, at least in my reading.

My time in Grinnell's ENG-207, _The Craft of Creative Nonfiction_, also had positive effects on my writing.
