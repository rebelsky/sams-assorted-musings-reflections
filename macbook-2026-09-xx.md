As I suggested in [a musing from  few weeks ago](sink-2026-08-13), I have three laptops [1]. And, as I mentioned, I ended up with three laptops because my primary laptop was in the shop for around two months. Why three? Well, I had the broken one that I used as a desktop, but I needed one to take to campus. ITS didn't have a loaner, so I bought a used one (a 2019 model for $400 or so). 

Now I'm finally getting around to switching back to my primary laptop. Why did it take so long? After all, I got it back right at the end of 2025? It's complicated. Strike that. It's not's complicated. It's simple. There are a few reasons. First of all, setting up a new laptop takes time. A lot of time. Not only do I have to copy files, I have to install a ton of software. And it takes about a day to connect my email. I didn't have the energy (physical or mental) for a multi-day effort. I'd also convinced myself that I needed to write [syncronization software](sink-2026-08-13) before switching back. And, well, depression and anxiety.

In any case, I'm trying to get things working. And, wouldn't you know it, but I couldn't log in to my College laptop. It's wonderful when things go smoothly [2]. Amazingly, it only took a few minutes with ITS to get it working again. I believe the issue was mostly that I hadn't logged in in a long time, which screws things up for our strange double-login process [3]. Even with the fix, login is still iffier than I'd like.

It appears that I'd done some of the painful setup a few months ago. At least the application ribbon was on the right side of the screen where I like it, homebrew seemed to be installed, and I'd even set up mail. Maybe all I really had left was moving files. Strange. On the other hand, I almost always encounter some issues.

Now that we're a few weeks later, I can verify that there were "some" issues. Perhaps many. Were I a more responsible muser, I might have recorded things as I went. I didn't. Here's what I recall.

Continue to figure out which files should be copied automatically.
 
Fix email.

Reconfigure browsers. Home page. Behavior upon restart. Where to download. Stuff like that.

Reconfigure email. I'd added the primary account, but I hadn't set the layout, the composing rules, the signatures, and things like that.

Install software as needed. I recall installing Microsoft Edge [4], Craft.do, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Steam [5], pandoc. What else? I don't don; as I said, I didn't keep a list. Deciding how to install software can also be fun. Do I use Homebrew? The App Store? Do I download an installer from the Web? Ideally, I should also check whether the software has been approved for use on College laptops, or at least whether I've told ITS that I'm installing it. I keep a list. I wonder if they do.

Update the software used to open different file types. For example, I prefer to open `.md` files in TextEdit rather than BBEdit. Well, to be honest, I prefer `vi` (or `vim` or `neovim`) in a terminal window. However, I sometimes need to copy the whole document, and TextEdit is a quick way to do so.

Configure `git`. I can't believe I hadn't done that already.

Try to figure out why some software seems to be mis-reporting data. For example,  Messages keeps telling me that I have unread messages (by including a number over its icon), but I don't.
