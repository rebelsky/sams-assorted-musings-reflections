A week ago, we'd just wrapped up the fifth week of classes for Fall 2026. Unfortunately, things are not going uniformly well in CSC-151, our introductory course. While many students are making good progress, even when they face seemingly complicated issues like higher-order programming, others are struggling with the basics of the course. There are always some students who struggle. This semester, there are many more and they are struggling more than in the past.

In a few cases, I can identify potential reasons. Perhaps they haven't been doing the readings. If you don't read the material, it is hard to understand. Perhaps they've been facing unexpected personal challenges and haven't been giving the class the attention it needs. Perhaps they've been ill and have missed too many classes. Lots of things interfere.

But there are also too many cases in which I know the students are working hard, and, for reasons I can't necessarily determine, things just aren't clicking. There are some common struggles I see. Many don't see the patterns in the problems. Many don't seem to have built a framework for the ideas that they can build upon.

Things came to a head last Friday, which is quiz day in the class. On Fridays, students get quizzes on new topics, but also get a chance to take makeup quizzes on topics they have not yet mastered. Far too many students looked frustrated at probems old and new. Far too many students didn't even want to attempt the old topics.

Here's where things stood after I'd graded week five's learning assessments (LAs).

* 12 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of procedural abstraction (week 1)
* 9 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of decomposition (week 1)
* 22 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of tracing (week 2)
* 24 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of anonymous procedures (week 3)
* 21 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of conditionals (week 4)
* 52 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of lists (with map, filter,
  and reduce) (week 5)
* 8 students had not yet demonstrated mastery of collaboration (week 5)

Those first three topics very much worried me.

While Ms. Lane and her breakers of charts were referring to love, their suggestion also applies to teaching (and so many other issues). I can't ignore the problem. So, throughout the day, I tried to figure out what I might do. I don't like seeing students struggle. I detest seeing so many students struggle.

I'm teaching the class much like I've done in past semesters; the only major changes has been from DrRacket to Scamper. And, while Scamper sometimes behaves strangely, that shouldn't be an issue. Students should be much the same; while Covid changed student experiences, I've taught post-covid students. Grinnell's test-optional policies have also been in place for about five years. I can't tell what's caused the change, but there's been a change.

By the end of the day, I came to a conclusion. There were enough students struggling that they needed more than our standard support structures: evening tutors, mentor sessions, individual tutors, individual meetings with the instructor, support from the Academic Success Center. So I decided that I'm going to try something different. Next week is going to be a review week. I will plan a structured path through the past five weeks of material. Students will get support through class sessions. They will, of course, have to engage carefully with the material, including memorizing more "vocabulary" (names and purposes of procedures) than they have, but I don't want to leave so many students behind.

What about the students who aren't struggling? I'm working to find a fun topic, such as animation or reactive programming, that is not core to the course and that could give them an enjoyable one-week project. We'll see if I succeed.
